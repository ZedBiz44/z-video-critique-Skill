# Video Review Checklists

## Level One Fast Attention Reel

- Does the opening immediately signal a relevant problem, desire, mistake, tension, consequence, or curiosity for the intended viewer?
- Does the hook create a promise the rest of the reel actually pays off?
- Is there one main useful point, or has the script crammed several lessons, features, or claims into a few seconds?
- Does the middle earn trust with something useful, believable, or specific rather than a list of unsupported claims?
- Does the call to action follow naturally from the video?
- Does the voice or presenter change pace, emphasis, emotion, expression, or energy at the important moments instead of staying flat and merely polished?
- Do visual changes, B-roll, screenshots, graphics, reactions, props, crops, or cutaways each serve at least one purpose: clarify, prove, intensify, refresh attention, break monotony, improve rhythm, or add relevant personality?
- Were weak generated openings, endings, dead holds, or unstable frames trimmed instead of preserved just because the provider created them?
- Is the reel simple enough to remember after one viewing?

For this format, a missing long-form treatment is not a defect. Too many ideas usually is.

Use only the sections relevant to the supplied video and review job. These are judgment prompts, not a reason to invent defects or produce a long audit.

## Message and Story

Check whether the intended viewer can understand the message, whether the opening earns attention without misleading, whether scenes follow a clear order, whether proof supports the claim, and whether the requested customer action is understandable. Flag important information that is missing, repeated, or contradicted.

Do not force one sales formula onto every training, story, demonstration, identity, or awareness video.

## Pacing and Attention

Check for dead time, rushed sections, awkward pauses, scenes that are too short to understand, or scenes that linger after their job is complete. Judge whether visual changes support the spoken message and whether the pace fits the stated audience and channel.

Fast cuts are not automatically better. Movement must serve the message.

## Visual Quality and Relevance

Check composition, crop, lighting, contrast, subject focus, visual-message fit, style consistency, and continuity. Flag irrelevant footage, fake interfaces presented as real, or visible generated-media defects such as warped hands, faces, tools, lettering, logos, shadows, reflections, or object motion.

Recommend real screenshots, recordings, or footage only when they would materially improve credibility or understanding.

## Motion and Editing

Check natural movement, believable animation, useful background motion and depth, presenter placement and scale, smooth and suitable transitions, continuity across scenes, and whether repeated static boxes or basic fades make the video feel like a slide presentation.

Flag distracting effects or decorative motion only when they compete with the message.

## Presenter, Character, and Lip-Sync

Check the correct person, character, or avatar against supplied references. Review face, hair, clothing, expression, eye direction, hands, body movement, mouth movement, presenter scale, and placement. Identify when a supposed animated scene is effectively static.

Do not claim exact identity, voice, or character approval without an approved comparison source.

## Narration and Audio

Check narration clarity, continuity, pacing, completeness, and audible consistency. Listen for cut-off words, speech spilling across scene boundaries, voice changes, poor lip-sync, clipping, distortion, noise, unwanted silence, low volume, duplicate audio, unwanted generator audio, and abrupt starts, stops, or fades.

Judge music and effects against narration clarity and the intended emotional job. Critique reports the audible result; production owns repair and technical measurement.

## Captions, Text, and Brand

Check exact wording, spelling, price, URL, name, offer, and customer action against supplied approved sources. Review caption timing, line breaks, reading speed, normal-size legibility, safe placement, text-subject overlap, logo shape and visibility, and consistent approved colours and typography.

Do not invent a brand rule, safe area, font, type size, or colour value that was not supplied or verified. Generated or distorted text should normally be replaced with an editable production layer.

## Final Viewing Quality

Check the actual reviewed file for black, frozen, corrupt, or missing sections; clipped edges; unsafe crops; visible scaling or compression damage; wrong orientation; unexpected blank frames; missing or duplicate audio; and incomplete openings or endings.

Do not claim that codecs, file size, platform specifications, links, legal rights, accessibility, attachment handling, or delivery passed unless each item was actually checked.

## Successful-Video Analysis

Observe the hook, promise, story order, pacing, presenter, demonstrations, proof, visuals, editing rhythm, captions, audio, emotional movement, and customer action. Identify patterns that could be tested without copying distinctive wording, footage, characters, branding, music, protected material, or a shot-for-shot structure.

Separate what is visible from what is known about performance. High views alone do not prove strong watch time, qualified attention, leads, sales, or profit.

## Comparison Review

Compare both videos against the same intended job when possible. Identify the most important difference first. Preserve what already works in the ZedBiz video. Recommend only reference patterns that fit the ZedBiz audience, brand, offer, and purpose.

Do not treat every difference as a weakness or copy a reference simply because it performed well in a different context.
